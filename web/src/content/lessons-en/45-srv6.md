Segment Routing encodes the path in the packet: the source decides which segments to traverse, and intermediate nodes forward by segment identifier. **SRv6** uses IPv6 addresses as segment identifiers (SIDs), removing the dependency on MPLS labels and fitting cloud-native, programmable transport.

## Three core concepts

- **Locator**: an IPv6 prefix identifying a node/location in the SRv6 domain;
- **SID**: a specific segment inside the locator, such as `2001:db8:100::1`; common behaviors include `End` and `End.DX6`;
- **Segment List**: the SID sequence written by the source, representing an explicit path.

**BE (best effort)** specifies a single remote SID: the ingress encapsulates the original packet in an outer IPv6 header (destination = SID), the underlay forwards by ordinary IPv6 routing, and the egress recognizes its local SID, decapsulates, and delivers. **Policy** uses a segment list for multi-segment paths, traffic engineering, and slicing.

## SRv6 BE in this simulator

```
srv6 locator 2001:db8:100::/64
srv6 sid 2001:db8:100::1
srv6 route 2001:db8:2::/64 sid 2001:db8:200::1
```

- The ingress matches the business prefix in `srv6 route` and encapsulates an outer IPv6 header (destination = remote SID);
- The underlay uses IPv6 static/OSPFv3 routes to deliver the outer packet;
- The peer sees the destination SID in its local locator/SID, decapsulates, and forwards the inner IPv6 packet.

Inspect with `display srv6` / `show srv6`. A capture shows the outer Locator/SID addresses and the inner original ICMPv6 packet between PCs.

## Relationship to MPLS/SR-MPLS

- **SR-MPLS**: the SID is an MPLS label and requires a label stack;
- **SRv6**: the SID is an IPv6 address, enabling stronger programmability, network slicing, and service chaining;
- **TI-LFA**: topology-independent loop-free backup with sub-second failover;
- **Evolution**: existing MPLS VPNs can coexist with SRv6, encapsulating at ingress/egress during migration.
